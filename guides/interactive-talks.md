# Present an interactive research tool

Use this route when the interface and the exploration it enables are part of the contribution. The paper establishes the claims; the tool demonstrates the experience. Do not replace the latter with a sequence of static paper screenshots.

## Build one investigation

Choose a real example that leads to a useful observation. Keep its input, selected item, and values consistent through the sequence:

1. **Motivate the task.** State the practical difficulty directly. If equations are hard to interpret, typeset the relevant terms at readable scale rather than showing miniature paper pages.
2. **Orient briefly.** Show the actual workspace and name only the views needed for this example. Small interface text here is context, not a reading assignment.
3. **Act and focus.** Show a purposeful selection, click, expansion, or drag. Enlarge the resulting evidence while retaining a recognizable selected item, connection, or nearby boundary.
4. **Hold and interpret.** Let the audience read the important value or change. Explain why it matters before moving to another component.
5. **Broaden carefully.** Compare another method or expose a shared structure after the example gives those concepts meaning. End with the capability the audience has actually seen.

A single large demo viewport with one timely caption is a useful default. Add a schematic or enlarged equation when it explains a mechanism the interface cannot show clearly. Keep the demonstration centered on what the system does; do not let a generic schematic become the main visual while the real tool is reduced to a thin strip.

## Borrow interaction principles from references

Useful references include [Transformer Explainer](https://poloclub.github.io/transformer-explainer/), [CNN Explainer](https://poloclub.github.io/cnn-explainer/), and [Diffusion Explainer](https://poloclub.github.io/diffusion-explainer/). Inspect the current interface before copying a particular behavior; these links are optional references, not build dependencies.

The transferable patterns are:

- Expand the selected component while keeping its inputs and outputs recognizable.
- Carry one semantic color through a label, value, and connection. Preserve the tool's own data encoding; a zero or neutral result must not acquire a positive color just to match a slide accent.
- Reveal a concrete numerical mechanism before introducing its abstract vocabulary.
- Compare one change in a stable frame. Keep common controls and unchanged components aligned so the contrast is visible.

These are design decisions to adapt, not evidence that a new presentation improves learning. Record a reference's reading order and interaction logic rather than copying its decorations.

## Keep evidence and production distinct

Use actual tool outputs and controls. Record the source state and selection along with the capture. A walkthrough may compose lossless UI states, cursor actions, and camera motion; label that method accurately in production records. Do not invent a control, result, or tested workflow. For example, an interface comparison of objective structure does not establish a controlled comparison between training runs.

Read the original result's scope before simplifying it. A logged zero contribution for a selected token does not imply that every prompt in its training step contributes zero; rounded display values may not support literal arithmetic on the shown fraction.

## Check the explanation before polishing motion

For each speaking beat, identify the visible evidence and the sentence it supports. Core reading text must meet the [visual profile](visual-design.md) at its final on-slide size; pixel resolution alone is insufficient. Keep an overview short, focus before asking the audience to read, and hold after the interaction long enough to understand its effect.

Use [spoken notes](spoken-notes.md) for intent → action → change → meaning. Use [motion](motion.md) for source density, camera ownership, easing, crop boundaries, and encoded/native playback checks. Those production details belong in the scene and review files, not in the words the speaker reads.
