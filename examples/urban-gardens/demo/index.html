Smarter Decisions for Urban Gardens

Static previews. Native Keynote playback and presenter-notes appearance need a separate review.

01 · garden-promise

Spoken notes

Hi, I’m your presenter. By the end of this short example, you’ll see how a collection of sensor readings can become a decision someone can actually use. Let’s start with a familiar moment: you arrive at a garden, and several beds seem to need attention.

02 · garden-question

Spoken notes

Where do you start? A dashboard full of numbers may not help you choose. For this example, the goal is simple: help a gardener decide which bed to check first. That gives us a clear question to organize the whole talk around.

03 · garden-framework

Spoken notes

The idea has three connected steps. First, observe what’s happening. Next, use those observations to make a decision clear. Then, let the gardener act and give feedback. The animation keeps those steps connected before opening them into the work inside each one.

04 · garden-roadmap

Spoken notes

Here’s the route through the idea. We begin with soil and weather readings. Then we look at how to explain a priority. Finally, we leave room for the gardener to correct the system. Each step has one job, and together they support a useful decision.

05 · garden-comparison

Spoken notes

Look at the two versions here. The left gives us a number, but it doesn’t tell us what to do. The right points to a next step and the context we should check. These are made-up examples; the point is the difference in what each version helps someone decide.

06 · garden-uncertainty

Spoken notes

Of course, a clear recommendation can still be wrong. So the interface should show what it used, flag readings that need another look, and ask before doing something hard to undo. That makes uncertainty something people can work with.

07 · garden-evaluation

Spoken notes

How would we test this idea? These three columns give us a starting point: usefulness, reliability, and control. We would check whether people can choose a task, whether the advice survives a faulty sensor, and whether they can correct a bad suggestion. These are evaluation questions, not reported results.

08 · garden-takeaway

Spoken notes

To wrap up this example, the goal is to make the next action easier to understand. Connect a reading to a decision. Show the reason and the uncertainty. And keep people involved in what happens next. Those three ideas give the talk a clear ending.

Spoken notes
